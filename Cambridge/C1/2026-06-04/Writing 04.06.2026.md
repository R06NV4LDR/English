After a recent lecture,  the proposed meat ban from the canteen has come to the forefront of public debate. While some argue that banning meat is paramount, others emphasize the significance of meat in our nutrition.
This essay will analyze two potential approaches to this issue and determine which is most effective.

On the one hand, it is widely recognized that meat is a huge driver of climatic change. Furthermore is the well being for these animals not always provided as it should be. 
Therefore one could argue that a ban from our canteen would be a statement against the meat industry's current course. 

On the other hand, a more compelling argument can be made against the ban. Humans are omnivores since thousands of years. Although there's been a trend around vegetarianism lately, a majority of people still eats meat and isn't thinking about quitting it.
Another reason against it is the personal choice, students stated that it's not in their interest to renounce   their meat-based diet. Even if that would mean to eat somewhere else in the future.

In conclusion, while both, the ban supporters and the opposing side, have valuable arguments for their view on the topic, it is my opinion that removing meat from the canteen is counterproductive.
I'd like to acknowledge that it's vital to overthink the whole meat production process from the roots. A responsible dealing with the matter would be beneficial for animals, humans and the whole environment.
But If people can't get what they want for lunch, they'll go to eat somewhere else. This won't help the canteen in any way. As with everything in a democratic country, it should be everyone's free choice.
